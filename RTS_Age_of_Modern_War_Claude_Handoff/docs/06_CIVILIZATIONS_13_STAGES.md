Thirteen-Stage Civilization Bible — v1.3
Coverage: Exactly 13 stages, applicable to all game styles and to autonomous human and machine civilizations. 
Approval status: 13-stage count and deep progression across every stage are approved. Exact stage names and the 
specific content below are working design proposals. First playable content target: Stages 7-9, with Stage 8 as the 
focus; all other stages are represented in the authoritative registry but not advertised as playable yet.
1. Progression Philosophy
1.1 What a stage is
A stage reflects demonstrated civilization-scale capabilities across production, infrastructure, institutions, education, 
resource security, health, energy, logistics and military organization. It is not a global damage multiplier, and two Stage 
8 civilizations can have radically different armies and economies. Human and machine civilization branches can look 
fundamentally different at an equivalent numbered level.
1.2 State dimensions
For each civilization maintain recognized stage, actual capability scores or evidence by domain, scientific knowledge, 
designs/prototypes, operating industrial tooling, material input access, trained staff or robotic controller capacity, live 
power and transport networks, health/services, military doctrine and historic advancements or regressions. A looted 
advanced weapon may be used if supported but does not count as a reproducible industrial capability.
1.3 Mode-specific progression
Classic Skirmish uses explicit prerequisite cards and manual Advance Stage once gates are satisfied; speed/cost 
ceilings/starting stages are customizable and Stage 7-9 content is the first enabled range. Living World develops 
organically and may later recognize/proclaim a new stage; catastrophic disruptions can reduce effective capacity and 
possibly recognized stage without erasing all historical research. Machine civilizations meet analogous maturity gates 
through energy, fabrication, redundancy and network control rather than household food/population metrics.
1.4 Cross-stage asymmetry
Territory, resource reserves, doctrine, morale, terrain, intelligence, logistics, allies and training still matter. Advanced 
platforms have maintenance and energy costs, vulnerability to network disruption, transport chokepoints and scarcity. 
Stage 13 is powerful, not an automatic win state.
2. Universal Stage Registry and Individual Stage Cards
Stage 01 — Scattered Survivors
Civilization identity: Mobile survival bands; low trust beyond kin and small teams.
Architecture and infrastructure: Lean-tos, sealed rooms, camp stoves, hidden caches.
Economy and industry: Hand scavenging, barter, salvage sorting; supplies perish.
Military and warfare: Found-object weapons, few reliable rounds, avoidance, scouting and ambush.
Science, medicine and education: Foraging, water treatment, rudimentary medicine, basic repair.
Transportation and supply: Foot travel, carts, trails; no guaranteed supply chain.
Governance and civic systems: Temporary leadership, family obligation, personal reputation.
Environmental interaction: Avoid contamination, seek weather shelter, decontaminate water.

4. Dedicated First-Playable Progression: Stages 7–9
Stage 7 to 8: Mechanized State → Modern Nation
Demonstrate stable industrial chain from ore/salvage → alloy/components → vehicle assembly; maintain reliable 
power/fuel; train professional soldiers/officers; establish functioning medical and communication support; research 
integrated manufacturing/combined-arms methods. Unlock eligibility to modern manufacturing lines, advanced 
armored units, integrated comms, aviation and improved support. Does not spawn vehicles or auto-stock fuel.
Stage 8 to 9: Modern Nation → Networked Power
Demonstrate electronics/computing supply, mature recon assets, robust communication redundancy and trained 
engineering operators. Research digital networks, target-data fusion and electronic resilience. Unlock eligibility to 
advanced drones, networked sensors, electronic warfare, precision fire support and predictive logistics. Disruptible links 
and critical electronic components remain weaknesses.
Side-by-side player vs enemy
A human industrial faction may reach Stage 9 by sturdy electronic manufacturing and hardened supply, a conventional 
human faction via superior integration/doctrine, and a machine faction via distributed autonomy and fabrication. All 
use the same recognized stage ID but receive distinct available recipes, units, communications and AI priorities.
5. Specialization Tracks Across All Ages
Infrastructure: shelters → towns → utilities → industrial/modern systems → autonomous, resilient and planetary 
infrastructure. Military: bands → militia → professional conventional forces → sensor networks and synthetic units. 
Economy: salvage/barter → trade/industry → globalized manufacture → autonomous circular industry. Society: local 
trust → institutions → modern civic services → complex governance of synthetic/distributed life. Ecology: survival 
mitigation → regulation → restoration → planetary intervention, always with costs.
6. Design and Implementation Invariants
Stage IDs remain 1–13 and are never reused; names may be localized or renamed independently.
Stage eligibility is not currently stocked equipment, researched knowledge or working factories.
Unavailable stage content is explicitly disabled in first-playable skirmish.
Advancement requires real conditions and a validated command/event; both human and machine paths can reach 
equal ID with different gates.
Regression affects capabilities; losses do not magically delete historic knowledge without explicit causation.
Changing a simulation fidelity tier cannot change civilizational stage or duplicate materials.
All three initial factions have distinct Stage 7/8/9 content; no clone roster with only color changes.
Numeric prerequisite thresholds are tunable engineering/balance fixtures, not historical scientific claims.

## Operational use and unresolved advancement ambiguity

Exactly IDs 1–13 are fixed; displayed names are working labels. Each card preserves identity, architecture, economy, military, science, transport, governance, ecology, machine analogue, evidence and specialization from the PDF. G3 implements 7–9; G6 deepens the remainder. The source provides conceptual depth, not final recipes, numerical prerequisites or exhaustive faction rosters.

Do not infer a Stage 14 from the Stage 13 evidence field. See CON-04 for the card/matrix offset. Dedicated §4 is the strongest source for prototype 7→8→9 planning, with actual numeric gates still unresolved. Store recognized stage, knowledge, available tooling, running production and equipment inventory separately. A looted MBT is not proof that a Stage 3 settlement can manufacture MBTs.

For future expansion of each card, record: faction-specific institutions and prerequisites; functioning production graph; civilian needs; energy and materials; available military roles and counters; research vs tooling; transport dependencies; regression behavior; AI planning changes; UI explanations; assets; save migration; positive/negative stage tests. This is an authoring checklist, not invented canon.
