# Planetary and stellar engineering
Planned G21 extension of existing construction, space technology and autonomous command.
Planet projects survey → feasibility → reserve → build → transform → stabilize → maintain. Model atmosphere, pressure, temperature, weather, water, soil, radiation, geology and species-specific habitability as bounded game variables. Region-level changes visible in RTS; aggregate distant work. Recovery can fail or reverse if support is lost; damage and reconstruction persist. Start one world at a time; research, energy, fleet capacity and logistics unlock parallel projects and system automation.
Dyson swarms are modular collectors; spheres are fictional advanced projects with phased assembly, energy routing, maintenance and vulnerabilities. Stellar foundries, assembly arrays, atmospheric engines, orbital fabricators, reconstruction networks, civilization vaults, defense networks, mobile habitats, biosphere engines and system assembly networks provide actual services. Artificial worlds, rings, hollow worlds, orbital cities and mobile habitats use modular regions and existing combat/transport interfaces. Late stellar stabilization, material extraction, relocation and system assembly are fictional abstractions with explicit feasibility and costs.
Engineering fleet templates cover surface, atmosphere, orbital, stellar, fabrication, ecology, restoration, seeding, research and command. Assign priority/authority; AI plans routes, supply, work and defense; manual takeover reconciles reservations. Factions share rules but differ in technology, doctrine and assets.
Acceptance: first planet visibly transforms; later network accelerates without resource creation; interrupted projects resume; capture/damage changes service availability; artificial world supports valid regions and combat; relocation preserves IDs and dependent infrastructure; save/reload and deterministic replay match.
